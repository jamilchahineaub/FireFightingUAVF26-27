# CL-415 UAV: aircraft model and flight controller

What the simulations and the autopilot use for this aircraft, with the values and where each one comes from. Generated by `docs/make_model_doc.py` from the project data files, so the numbers here are the ones in the models. Axes: the CAD, Gazebo and this document use x forward, y left, z up (REP-103) with the origin at the wing leading edge on the centreline; the team MATLAB file (`flight_params/cl415_params.yaml`) and PX4 use x forward, y right, z down (FRD), which flips the sign of y, z and of Ixz's definition as noted there. Chord positions are quoted in percent of the mean aerodynamic chord (MAC) from the leading edge.

## 3. Is the aircraft balanced? CG, centre of lift, neutral point, thrust line

Longitudinal positions along the chord (full tank unless stated):

| point | x [m] | % MAC | note |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| wing aerodynamic centre (centre of lift of the wing alone) | -0.0694 | 25.0 | quarter chord; the wing's pitching moment about it is constant (Cm_ac = -0.105 from XFOIL) |
| centre of gravity, full | -0.0754 | 27.1 | SolidWorks |
| centre of gravity, empty | -0.0779 | 28.0 | SolidWorks |
| neutral point (centre of lift of the whole aircraft), full | -0.1517 | 54.6 | AVL, wing + tail + hull |
| neutral point, empty | -0.1498 | 53.9 | AVL |
| static margin, full / empty |  | 27.4 / 25.9 | -Cm_alpha / CL_alpha, the same as NP minus CG |

The CG is 6 mm behind the wing's own centre of lift, so the wing alone would be slightly unstable, which is normal for a tailed aircraft; the tail (tail volume 0.92) moves the aircraft's centre of lift back to 55 % MAC, 27 % of the chord behind the CG. Positive static margin means a nose-up disturbance produces a nose-down moment: the aircraft is statically stable in both loading cases, and the team MATLAB check and the PX4 flights confirm it dynamically (short period damping ratio 0.58, every mode Level 1 except the slow spiral). The margin is on the large side (10 to 20 % is the usual design range for a UAV of this kind): it costs a little elevator and trim drag (the trim elevator runs from +0.9 deg at cruise to about -4 deg at 1.1 Vs) and makes the aircraft steady rather than agile, which suits a water bomber. The CG could move aft to about 40 % MAC (x = -0.110 m) before the margin drops to 15 %; moving the battery is the lever for that, as in `mass/report.md`.

Vertical arrangement and the moments it creates:

| item | value | effect |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| wing chord plane above the CG | 101 mm (full), 66 mm (empty) | lift and drag act above the CG: a small pendulum stability and a nose-up moment from drag |
| thrust line above the CG | 161 mm (full), 126 mm (empty) | power pitches the nose down: 15.0 Nm at full static thrust, 1.28 Nm (Cm -0.027, 0.7 deg of elevator) at cruise |
| horizontal tail above the CG | 228 mm | keeps the tail above the spray and the prop wash |
| keel below the CG | 209 mm | water resistance acts low: full-power hull drag at the hump adds another nose-down moment on the water |
| main step aft of the CG | 65 mm, 17 deg behind the vertical through the CG | flying-boat practice puts the step 10 to 15 deg aft of the CG so the hull trims bow-up on the step; this hull is at the aft end of that range and planed cleanly in the sim |
| floating attitude at rest | draft 81 mm, 3.4 deg bow up, 2.7 deg heel onto one float | the centre of buoyancy sits under the CG; the hull alone is unstable in roll, as on the real aircraft, and leans on a float |

How these positions are carried into the simulators:

- Gazebo: the aerodynamic forces act at `cp` = the full-tank CG (-0.0754, 0, -0.1007) with the AVL moment coefficients taken about that same point, which is equivalent to lift acting at the neutral point with the wing's own moment; the inertial frame of `base_link` plus the servo, propeller and camera links reproduce the SolidWorks totals (composite mass 10.510 kg, CG x -0.0755 m, z -0.1003 m against 10.500 kg, -0.0754, -0.1007); the IMU sits at the CG; the thrust is applied at each hub with its reaction torque.
- Team MATLAB model: moments about the loaded CG with `r_thrust_from_loaded_cg_frd` for the thrust line, and the CG offset of the unloaded case through `mass_properties.cad`.
- PX4 does not need the positions; it sees their effect as trim. In the sim the cruise elevator is 0.8 deg trailing edge up at 0.7 deg pitch, where the static balance without the thrust line predicts 0.9 deg trailing edge down; 0.7 deg of the difference is the thrust-line moment, the remaining degree is the elevator's own lift and the fitted stall curve in the plugin. Both are within the first turn of a trim tab.

## 4. Aerodynamic model

AVL 3.52 vortex lattice on the CAD geometry (wing with NACA 4417 camber and the XFOIL lift slope, tail, hull and nacelles as slender bodies), XFOIL 6.99 for the section data, drag build-up by component. All derivatives per radian, body angle of attack measured from the hull datum (the wing sits at +2.65 deg), rates as p b/2V, q c/2V, r b/2V. Moments about the full-tank CG; the empty-CG set is in `flight_params/README.md`. The control derivatives are AVL's times the XFOIL viscous flap factor (0.83 aileron, 0.90 elevator, 0.83 rudder).

| CD0 | 0.03278 | CY_r | 0.2022 | CY_dr | 0.2221 |

Stall: CL_max 1.236 at 7.7 deg body alpha (critical section method: the first wing strip reaches the XFOIL cl_max at the stall Reynolds number), stall speed 13.9 m/s full, 11.8 m/s empty, best L/D 14.2 at 16.1 m/s. Signs follow the team convention: elevator and ailerons positive trailing edge down (Cm_de < 0, Cl_da > 0 for left down / right up), rudder positive trailing edge left (Cn_dr < 0), sideslip positive from the right.

Two implementations use these numbers. The team MATLAB model applies them directly with a sigmoid stall blend. Gazebo's `AdvancedLiftDrag` has a fixed-width blend, so for it the lift curve is refitted: CL0 0.544, CL_alpha 6.090, blend centre 14.4 deg, which matches AVL within 0.012 in CL from -5 to 6 deg and peaks at CL 1.236 at 10.6 deg. Each control surface is a separate entry (per degree of joint angle), the ailerons split antisymmetrically with their own small pitching-moment contribution.

## 5. Propulsion model

Baseline hardware from the builders' thread for this airframe: Hacker A50-16S V4 (365 kV, 21 mOhm, 1.8 A no-load) on 6S 16 Ah, 13.5x10.5-4 (scaled 155x12-4) four-blade scale propeller, counter-rotating (left CW, right CCW seen from behind). The model is a DC-motor equation (Kv, Rm, I0, pack sag, ESC 0.97) in balance with APC's published Ct and Cp tables for the prop, scaled to the 13.5 in diameter the hull clearance allows (`flight_params/propulsion/prop_tools.py`). Both simulators read the same maps.

|  | value |
| --- | ---: |
| static thrust, both motors | 93.2 N (T/W 0.90) |
| full-throttle thrust at 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 m/s | 88 / 82 / 73 / 63 N |
| thrust at 0.55 throttle, 10 / 15 / 20 m/s | 23.4 / 16.0 / 7.4 N |
| cruise 20 m/s, full tank | 7.96 N at throttle 0.556 |
| team momentum model T = a dT^2 - b dT V | a = 102.2 N, b = 1.92 N s/m (fit rms 2.5 N) |
| motor time constant | 0.035 s (0.04 s up / 0.06 s down in Gazebo) |
| thrust line | x = +0.265 m ahead of and 0.161 m above the full CG, hubs 0.2973 m from the centreline |

In Gazebo `libCl415PropellerMap.so` turns PX4's 0 to 1000 motor command into throttle, looks up thrust, shaft torque and rpm against the axial airspeed, applies the thrust at the hub and the reaction torque to the airframe. The team model uses the momentum fit above (`propulsion.momentum`). When different hardware is chosen, `flight_params/propulsion/evaluate.py --prop <apc file> --motor|--kv ...` regenerates every number in this section.

| motors | 0 to 1 | throttle | motor 1 left, motor 2 right, 1 to 1000 | dT 0 to 1 |

In Gazebo each surface is a revolute joint with a position PID (the gains come from the surface inertia and a 0.03 s target: elevator p 0.5155 i 1.65 d 0.01289, ailerons p 0.1691 i 0.5411 d 0.004228), with the hinge torque limited to 0.5 Nm. The team model uses first-order servos with tau 0.03 s and 500 deg/s rate limits, the same as `actuators` in the yaml.

## 7. Hull model (water)

`libCl415FlyingBoatHull.so` on the water variant, acting on the collision boxes of the hull, the floats and the wings (see `px4_sitl/README.md` for why Gazebo's own buoyancy and hydrodynamics plugins were not usable). Per box: exact clipping against the water plane for buoyancy, planing lift and bluff drag on the wetted bottom and sides, skin friction, form drag per group on the largest wetted cross-section, and heave damping. The bow boxes are tilted to represent the keel rocker, the afterbody boxes to the sternpost angle, and the afterbody runs in the wake hollow of the step once the hull planes.

## 8. Flight controller (PX4 v1.16.2, fixed-wing stack)

PX4's standard fixed-wing cascade is used unchanged; the airframe files `px4_sitl/airframes/4050_gz_cl415` (wheels) and `4051_gz_cl415_water` set its parameters from the model above.

**Rate loops** (`fw_rate_control`, 250 Hz): for each axis the normalised torque command is `tau = FF * rate_sp + P * (rate_sp - rate) + I * integral(rate_sp - rate)`, with P and I scaled by (V_trim/V)^2 and FF by V_trim/V so the surfaces do the same work at every airspeed. The feed-forwards are the steady-state surface deflection per unit body rate from the AVL derivatives at the 20 m/s trim speed, normalised by the full travel:

- roll: a steady roll rate p needs Cl_da * da = -Cl_p * p b/2V, so FF_roll = (-Cl_p b/2V) / (Cl_da * 25 deg) = 0.379
- pitch: a steady pull-up at rate q needs elevator for the pitch damping and for the extra angle of attack of the load factor, FF_pitch = ((-Cm_q c/2V) - Cm_alpha m V/(q S CL_alpha)) / (-Cm_de * 25 deg) = 0.525
- yaw: FF_yaw = (-Cn_r b/2V) / (-Cn_dr * 30 deg) = 0.180

| parameter | roll | pitch | yaw | meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| FF | 0.379 | 0.525 | 0.180 | feed-forward, from AVL |
| P | 0.08 | 0.12 | 0.08 | rate error gain, moderate starting values |
| I | 0.1 | 0.15 | 0.05 | integral, trims the surfaces (limit FW_PR_IMAX 0.4) |

**Attitude loop** (`fw_att_control`): `rate_sp = (angle_sp - angle) / TC` with the time constant `FW_P_TC` 0.4 s on the runway variant and 0.25 s on the water variant, roll TC 0.4 s, plus the coordinated-turn yaw rate g tan(phi) cos(theta) / V. Limits: bank 40 deg, pitch -20 to +25 deg, pitch rate 60 deg/s.

**Energy and path loops** (`fw_pos_control`): TECS trades altitude and airspeed with the throttle and the pitch setpoint (trim speed 20.0 m/s, minimum 16.7, stall 13.9, maximum 28.0, throttle trim 0.56, climb up to 5.0 m/s, sink 1.5 to 4.0 m/s, pitch offset 0.9 deg); NPFG lateral guidance (default 10 s period, 0.7 damping) turns the track error into a roll setpoint; waypoints are accepted at 25 m and loiters have 80 m radius.

**Control allocation** (`CA_AIRFRAME 1`, standard plane): the torque and thrust setpoints are distributed by the effectiveness matrix: left and right ailerons -0.5 / +0.5 of the roll torque, elevator 1.0 pitch, rudder 1.0 yaw, both motors 0.5 of the thrust. On the water variant the real lateral motor positions (+-0.297 m) are given to the allocator so differential thrust helps the rudder in yaw, which is what steers the hull before the rudder bites. The vertical offset of the thrust line is deliberately left out of the allocator: with it the allocator commanded full nose-down elevator against its own estimate of the thrust moment at take-off power; the moment is in the physics and the pitch integrator trims it.

**Take-off and landing logic**:

|  | runway | water | why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| method | runway take-off | runway take-off | heading hold with rudder (and differential thrust on the water), pitch held, rotation at FW_TKO_AIRSPD |
| taxi pitch RWTO_PSP | 2.0 deg | 12.0 deg | the hull needs the nose held up to climb onto the step |
| pitch trims | none | TRIM_PITCH 0.10, FW_DTRIM_P_VMIN 0.25 | the runway logic resets the integrators until lift-off, so on the water the loop needs the back pressure a pilot applies |
| rotation / climb-out | 16.7 m/s, min pitch 6.0 deg | 16.7 m/s, min pitch 8.0 deg | wheels: stern clearance 10 deg |
| approach | 5.0 deg at 16.7 m/s | 4.0 deg at 16.7 m/s | glassy-water approach is shallow |
| flare | from 1.5 m, 2.0 to 8.0 deg, sink 0.5 m/s | from 2.5 m, 4.0 to 9.0 deg, sink 0.4 m/s | touch on the step, nose up |
| terrain | FW_LND_USETER 0 | FW_LND_USETER 0 | no rangefinder yet, flare on GNSS/baro height |

**Estimation**: EKF2 with the Gazebo IMU at the CG, barometer, GNSS, the pitot and the magnetometer plugin; over a full circuit the attitude error against the simulator truth is 0.4 deg roll, 0.8 deg pitch, 2.2 deg yaw rms.

## 9. What the closed loops did

|  | runway flight | water flight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| take-off run | 36 m, 4.4 s, lift-off 19.0 m/s | 25 m, 4.8 s, lift-off 14.2 m/s |
| cruise throttle at 20 m/s (predicted 0.556) | 0.550 | 0.550 |
| cruise pitch / elevator | 0.7 deg / 0.8 deg TE up | 0.5 deg / 0.7 deg TE up |
| altitude tracking rms | 1.7 m | 1.3 m |
| airspeed tracking rms | 0.62 m/s | 0.51 m/s |
| touchdown | 0.64 m/s sink at 16.3 m/s, 1.3 deg nose up | 0.58 m/s sink at 14.4 m/s, stopped in 8.7 s |

Signs and authority of all four surfaces are confirmed by these loops: a wrong sign diverges, and the feed-forwards alone put the rate loops close to their setpoints. Open items are the roll overshoot in turns, the abrupt rotation off the water (22 deg pitch with the 12 deg taxi target and the trims carrying into the first second of flight) and the flare sink rate on the water, which comes out above the 0.4 m/s asked for.
